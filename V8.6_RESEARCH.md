# V8.6 — Prequential probability calibration and ranking audit

## Research question

V8.6 separates two questions that must not be conflated:

1. Does the V5 ordering of the 45 numbers rank the next draw's six main numbers better than a uniform 6/45 baseline?
2. Are the numerical marginal probabilities themselves well calibrated?

A probability calibration change cannot improve the top-k ranking when it is a monotone scalar temperature transform. It can only make probability values more or less confident.

## Locked evaluation design

- Score targets 752–1251 (500 draws), matching the recent V7/V8 historical window for comparability.
- Generate 200 earlier one-step-ahead forecasts (targets 552–751) as a calibration warm-up; do not include these rows in reported test metrics.
- Use a 500-draw V5 training window, minimum history 180, and ridge alpha 1.0.
- For each scored target, choose temperature from the predeclared grid [0.50, 0.67, 0.80, 1.00, 1.25, 1.50, 2.00, 3.00] by minimizing mean log loss over the immediately preceding 200 prequential forecast vectors only.
- For each forecast, re-solve one intercept so the 45 marginal inclusion probabilities sum exactly to six.
- Compare raw V5, rolling-temperature V5, frozen V4 long-frequency baseline, and uniform 6/45 probabilities.

## Metrics and inference

Proper scores: per-draw Brier and LogLoss. Ranking: actual hits in top 6, top 10, and top 15, mean rank of the six actual numbers, and mean percentile rank. Uniform expected top-k hits are 0.8, 1.3333, and 2.0 respectively.

Reliability: ten equal-count probability bins, predicted versus observed inclusion rate, and expected calibration error. These are descriptive diagnostics; inferential intervals are draw-clustered.

Five predeclared one-sided comparisons use 10-draw moving-block bootstrap and Holm correction: raw V5 Brier versus uniform, raw V5 LogLoss versus uniform, raw V5 top-6 hits versus the uniform expectation, rolling-temperature Brier versus raw V5, and rolling-temperature LogLoss versus raw V5.

## Interpretation limits

- The 752–1251 test window has already been examined by prior V7/V8 work; it is not a fresh untouched holdout.
- Temperature is selected only from earlier forecast outcomes. No target draw contributes to its own calibration.
- Temperature scaling preserves number ranking; any top-k difference between raw and calibrated outputs would indicate an implementation defect.
- A retrospective improvement is not proof of future lottery advantage.
- V8.6 does not alter the production picker, rewrite the manual CSV, or create or replace any forward lock.
- No official lottery website or API is accessed.
